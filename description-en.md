# Horror Banshee

**Don't stare.**

A woman stands alone between the trees. She looks harmless.
Then her face changes, just for the blink of an eye.

**Horror Banshee** brings a slowly unfolding horror encounter to Minecraft:
a human disguise, sobbing, an increasingly frantic run, and a scream before the hunt.

## An encounter that can begin with a glance

Right-click her, strike her, or keep your crosshair on her for more than five
seconds to trigger the reveal. Her human appearance returns between irregular
glimpses of something monstrous as she races around you and seeks cover among
the trees. When the scream comes, the chase begins.

## The Matriarch

A rare woman in a gothic gown with a genuinely three-dimensional, layered skirt.
Her final transformation lifts her above the ground. The scream pushes nearby
creatures away; low flights and melee approaches follow. With 160 health and
a boss bar, she demands more preparation than an ordinary Banshee.

## Explore. Listen. Survive.

- **Three original women**, with their outfits preserved in their transformed forms.
- **Haunted stretches of large taiga**, with progressive mist and long silences between rare whispers, humming and distant sobs.
- **The Banshee Crypt**, buried in a mountainside near water, with three seated inhabitants.
- **Collectible tears**, sometimes shed during the frantic run, and rare rewards after combat.
- **Two potions**, granting a brief Banshee or Matriarch disguise; the enchanted potion adds Strength and Regeneration.
- **Banshee Life Essence**, a reusable relic for one slow-flying Matriarch transformation each night, until dawn or voluntary cancellation.

Voices follow the encounter: quiet singing, sobbing, a transformation scream,
and a victory laugh if the Banshee kills you. Original pixel-art textures and
GeckoLib animations keep the characters rooted in Minecraft's visual style.

**Minecraft Java 1.21.1 · NeoForge · Java 21 · GeckoLib required**

Documented version: **0.11.1**. Install the mod and GeckoLib on both client and server.
